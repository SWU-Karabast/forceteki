Engine-level unknowns

General behaviours the rules do not state. Each is decided and tested once in the engine tests (engine-test-notes.md); per-card notes on the cards below do not repeat them.

1. Indirect damage and bases (Always Two only)

The question In Always Two, may the player who assigns indirect damage put more on a base than it can still take before reaching its health? If so, where does the excess go (it stays on the base)?

Why it was unclear The rules only cap damage assigned to units (a unit cannot be assigned more than its remaining HP). Nothing is said for bases. In Always Two a base can be damaged past its health without eliminating the player, so the question arises there. In Twin Suns a base at its health is eliminated, so it does not arise. It matters for cards that ask "if a base was damaged this way".

How we are handling it Open for Always Two only. Tested once at engine level; no per-card questions or notes about it.

2. "You take the initiative" when the teammate takes it (Always Two)

The question In Always Two the initiative is shared by the team. When your teammate takes the initiative, do triggers worded "when you take the initiative" (or "the next time you take the initiative") fire for you?

Why it was unclear The Doubles rules say both players have the initiative and that when one takes it neither can take it again, but not whether "you take" is personal. Cards worded "if you have the initiative" are a different case and are settled: the team's initiative counts for both teammates.

How we are handling it Open. Tested once at engine level; no per-card question.

3. "Defeat a base" in Always Two

The question What does an ability that defeats a base actually do in Always Two? For example, is damage set equal to the base's health, and what happens to the player?

Why it was unclear The rules define a defeated base only as damage at or above its health. In Twin Suns that eliminates the player. In Always Two a base at its health does not eliminate the player, and the amount of damage an ability places is not published.

How we are handling it Open for Always Two. Twin Suns is settled (elimination). Tested once at engine level.

4. "When you heal damage from your base" when a teammate does the healing

The question In Always Two, if your teammate's card or ability heals damage from your base, does that count as you healing it?

Why it was unclear "You" is personal, but the heal happens to your own base. No ruling covers a heal of your base caused by a teammate's effect.

How we are handling it Open for Always Two. Twin Suns: the heal of up to 5 for eliminating a player is real healing and triggers it for the damage actually healed.

5. Replacing a teammate's attacker

The question When your teammate's unit is attacking a base, can you use Asajj from your own hand to replace the attacker? If so, who controls Asajj as the new attacker (and so deals the combat damage)?

Why it was unclear "Friendly" includes the teammate, so the trigger probably applies. But Asajj comes from your hand under your control and takes the place of a unit you cannot attack with, and no rule says who controls the unit put into play as the attacker.

How we are handling it Open for Always Two. No per-card note on Asajj.

6. Impropriety Among Thieves: Always Two seating within a team

The question In Always Two, which player on each team has their teammate on their right and which has an enemy? In other words, which seat does each player take within their team?

Why it was unclear R8 says "the player to their right" is taken from each player's own perspective, so for one player on each team it is the teammate and for the other an enemy. It also says which seat each player takes within their team is not yet defined, so the exact mapping of who takes whose unit cannot be fixed.

How we are handling it Open. The card's notes describe the swap by each player's own right-hand neighbour; a scan for other seating-dependent cards is still to do.
